## The finding

Most email tools discount annual billing by 15–20%. AWeber's gap is much wider, and it is worth seeing the two columns side by side.

From AWeber's own pricing page, checked August 2026, at the 500-subscriber tier:

| Plan | Billed annually | Billed monthly | The gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lite** | **$12.49/mo** | **$15/mo** | +$2.51 (~20%) |
| **Plus** | **$19.99/mo** | **$30/mo** | **+$10.01 (~50%)** |
| Done For You | $20/mo + $79 setup | — | — |

**On the Plus plan, paying monthly costs half again as much as committing for a year.**

That is not hidden — both numbers are published. But the monthly price is the one you see when you sign up in a hurry, and the annual price is the one quoted in every comparison article.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

**Annual billing is a bet that you will still be using the tool in twelve months.** For an established business that is usually a safe bet and the discount is free money.

For someone launching, it is not. The whole reason to pay monthly is that you are not yet sure — and AWeber charges the most of any tool here for that uncertainty.

**Work out which you are before you reach the checkout.** If you are confident, take the annual price and AWeber is competitively priced. If you are not, add 50% to the number in the comparison article you read and see whether it still looks good.

## What you get for it

AWeber is a mature, dependable email platform. Its deliverability reputation is good, its support is better than most, and it has been doing this longer than almost anyone.

What it does not have is a reason to choose it over newer tools on features. The interface is dated. The automation is competent rather than deep. **Nothing here is bad; nothing is a differentiator either.**

The **Done For You** tier is the genuine oddity — $20/month plus a $79 one-time setup fee, where AWeber builds your initial setup for you. Very few competitors offer a human doing the configuration at that price, and for someone who dreads the blank screen it may be worth more than any feature comparison.

## Where the entry price lands

**$12.49/month for 500 subscribers** is not cheap for the ceiling. [MailerLite's free plan holds 250 subscribers and sends 2,500 emails a month for nothing](/reviews/mailerlite-review), and its paid entry is $12 with more headroom. [Kit is free to 10,000 subscribers](/reviews/convertkit-review) if you only need broadcasts.

**And there is no free plan here at all** — a 14-day trial, then payment. For a tool whose main argument is reliability rather than capability, starting the relationship with a card is a harder sell than it was when AWeber set the standard.

## Who should still choose it

**Anyone who values a phone number over a feature list.** AWeber's support is a real product attribute and it does not show up in pricing tables.

**Anyone who wants setup done for them.** The Done For You tier has few equivalents.

**Anyone billing annually who has already decided.** At $12.49 and $19.99 the pricing is fair; it is only the monthly column that is expensive.

## Who should not

**Anyone still testing the idea.** No free plan, a short trial, and the widest monthly premium in the category is the worst possible combination for someone who is not sure yet.

**Anyone who needs more than email.** [Systeme.io runs $17–97 with funnels, courses and affiliate management included, and a permanent free tier](/reviews/systeme-io-free-plan-limits). Different category of product, but if you are building a business rather than a mailing list, that is the comparison that matters.

## The honest summary

**AWeber is a fine product with an expensive way to pay for it.** Committed annually it is competitive. Paid monthly, Plus costs 50% more than the number you probably saw quoted.

Decide your billing cadence before you compare prices, because for this tool it changes the answer.
