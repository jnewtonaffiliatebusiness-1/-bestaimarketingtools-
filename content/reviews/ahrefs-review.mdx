## The plan table hides the number that matters

Here is what Ahrefs publishes, side by side.

| | Lite | Standard | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | **$129/mo** | **$249/mo** | **$449/mo** |
| Projects | 5 | 20 | 50 |
| Tracked keywords | 750 | 2,000 | 5,000 |
| Historical data | 6 months | 2 years | 5 years |
| Crawl credits | 100,000 | 500,000 | 1,500,000 |
| **Included users** | **1** | **1** | **1** |

Read that bottom row again. **It does not change.** Spending three and a half times as much buys you more data and no additional people.

## Price it per person

Extra seats are priced per plan: **$40/month each on Lite** (up to 2 more), **$60 on Standard** (up to 5 more), **$80 on Advanced** (up to 10 more).

So the real question is not which plan — it is how many people need to log in.

| Team size | Lite | Standard | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 person | $129 | $249 | $449 |
| 2 people | $169 | $309 | $529 |
| **3 people** | **$209** | **$369** | **$609** |
| 5 people | $289 | $489 | $769 |

**A three-person team on Standard is $369/month — $4,428 a year.** That is the figure to compare against alternatives, and it is 48% above the price on the pricing page.

Seat sharing is the obvious workaround and it is worth being clear-eyed about: it violates the terms, and it means your rank tracking and alerts belong to one login that one person controls. **Decide that consciously rather than drifting into it.**

## The two plans nobody quotes

Ahrefs publishes a **Starter plan at $29/month** and **Ahrefs Free**. Neither appears in most comparison articles, which reach for the $129 Lite figure because it is the first of the three main tiers.

If your actual need is checking a few dozen keywords and looking at competitor backlinks occasionally — which describes most people who buy SEO tools — **start at $29 and find out what you run out of.** The gap between $29 and $129 is $1,200 a year, and it is worth spending a month discovering whether you need it.

The honest caveat: those cheaper tiers exist precisely because the main ones are priced for agencies and in-house teams, and they will be thin if you are doing this daily.

## Lite's real weakness is time, not volume

Lite's constraint that catches people is not the 5 projects or the 750 keywords. It is **6 months of historical data**.

SEO work is mostly about direction — is this trending up, did that update hurt us, what did this look like a year ago. Six months of history means you cannot answer year-over-year questions at all, and you will not notice the gap until the moment you need it.

Standard's 2 years is the point where the historical view becomes genuinely useful. **If trend analysis is why you are buying, that upgrade is the one to make, and it is a data decision rather than a volume one.**

## The add-ons sit outside all of it

Beyond the plan and the seats, Ahrefs lists **Content Kit from $99/month**, **Report Builder at $99/month**, **Brand Radar AI from $199/month**, and custom prompt packages at **$50, $100 and $250/month** for 2,500, 7,000 and 25,000 checks respectively. Enterprise is **$1,499/month on an annual commitment**.

None of that is unusual for tooling at this level. It does mean the answer to "what does Ahrefs cost" is a sum of four things — plan, seats, add-ons, and any AI prompt package — rather than one figure.

## Who should not buy this

**If you publish and want to know what to write next**, an SEO suite at $129/month is a large purchase for a question that keyword research at $29 answers.

**If you have no site traffic yet**, tooling is not the constraint. Ahrefs measures results; it does not create them, and a rank tracker on a site with no rankings is an expensive way to watch a flat line.

## The honest summary

**Ahrefs is a serious tool and the data is the reason people pay for it.** The crawl credits and the index behind them are not marketing numbers.

**But the price is per person, not per plan, and every tier ships with one seat.** Work out your team size first, add $40 to $80 per extra head, and compare that total. Then check whether $29 Starter or the free tier answers your actual question before spending four figures a year on the answer.
