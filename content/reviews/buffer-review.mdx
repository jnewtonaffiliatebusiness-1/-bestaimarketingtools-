## Buffer's price is a multiplication, not a number

Almost every scheduling tool charges per user or per plan. Buffer charges **per channel** — one connected social account.

| Plan | Price | What it is per |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 3 channels included |
| **Essentials** | **$5/mo** | **per channel**, 1 user |
| **Team** | **$10/mo** | **per channel**, unlimited users |

So the $5 you see quoted is the price of scheduling to *one* account. Compare it to a flat-rate competitor and you are not comparing like with like until you have done the multiplication.

## Do the multiplication for your actual setup

A typical small business runs Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, a YouTube channel and TikTok. That is six.

| Channels | Essentials | Team |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | $15/mo | $30/mo |
| **6** | **$30/mo** | **$60/mo** |
| 10 | $50/mo | $100/mo |

**Six channels on Essentials is $360 a year, not $60.** Nothing is hidden here — Buffer states the model plainly — but the headline figure and your figure are different by however many accounts you run.

Buffer does apply a volume discount: channels 1–10 are at the standard rate, and **anything above 10 costs less per channel**. That helps agencies and does nothing for a business with six accounts.

## The free plan is better than its reputation, with one catch

Free gives you **3 channels**, **10 scheduled posts per channel**, **100 ideas**, **1 user account**, plus AI Assistant, Insights, community inbox and API access with one key and 3,000 requests a month.

The detail that changes how usable this is: the 10 scheduled posts **refill as they publish**. It is a rolling queue depth, not a monthly cap. Posting three times a week per channel, you will essentially never hit it.

**The catch is the single user.** Free supports one account, and Essentials is also single-user. The moment a second person needs to schedule anything, you are on **Team at $10 per channel** — double the per-channel rate, for a reason that has nothing to do with how much you post.

**Check whether anyone else touches your posting before pricing this.** It is the difference between $30 and $60 a month at six channels.

## What "unlimited posts" means on paid

Paid plans list unlimited scheduled posts per channel, with a stated fair use policy capping at **5,000 posts per channel**. That is not a limit anyone posting like a human will meet, and quoting it as a restriction would be dishonest. It exists to stop automated abuse.

Ideas also go from 100 on free to unlimited on paid. If you use Buffer as a content backlog rather than just a scheduler, that is a more meaningful upgrade than the post limit.

## What it does not do

Buffer is a **publishing** tool. It schedules, it queues, it reports on what you posted.

It does not do social listening. If you need to know when someone mentions your brand somewhere you are not looking, that is a different category of product and a considerably more expensive one. **Do not buy Buffer expecting to be alerted to anything.**

It also does not solve the problem most small businesses actually have, which is not scheduling — it is having something worth posting. A queue makes consistent publishing easy; it does not make the content work.

## Who this is right for

**It fits** a one-person or small operation running a handful of channels that wants posting off the daily to-do list, with an interface that does not require training. On three or four channels at $5 each, it is inexpensive and it is pleasant to use, which matters for a tool you open every day.

**It fits badly** if you run many channels for many clients — the per-channel model works against you until you pass ten — or if you need approval workflows and listening, where the heavier platforms earn their price.

## The honest summary

**Buffer's free plan is real, the rolling 10-post limit is more generous than it reads, and the tool is genuinely nice to use.**

**Just do the multiplication before you compare it to anything.** $5 is one channel with one user. Your number is $5 or $10 times the accounts you actually run, and for most small businesses that lands between $30 and $60 a month — a normal price for the category, and six to twelve times the one on the pricing page.
