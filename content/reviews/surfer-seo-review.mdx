## Put the two middle plans side by side

This is the whole review in one table. Surfer publishes four tiers, all priced billed-yearly:

| | Discovery | Standard | Pro | Peace of Mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | **$49/mo** | **$99/mo** | **$182/mo** | **$299/mo** |
| Documents | 120 | **360** | **360** | Unlimited* |
| Team seats | 1 | 3 | 5 | 10 |

Look at the document row across Standard and Pro. **It does not move.** Both plans give you 360 documents.

So the $83 a month between them — **$996 a year** — buys exactly two additional team seats. That is **$498 per seat per year**, and it is the single most important number on the pricing page, because nothing on the page says it out loud.

## Which number is your constraint?

Most people size an SEO content tool by output: how many articles a month do we optimise? That instinct sends you looking at the document column, where the meaningful jump is Discovery to Standard — 120 to 360, triple the volume for double the price. That upgrade is straightforwardly good value.

Above Standard, output stops being the question entirely. **Pro and Peace of Mind are seat purchases wearing plan names.**

The practical test is one question: *how many people need to open the tool?*

- **One person** — Discovery at $49 covers you until you exceed 120 documents.
- **Two or three** — Standard at $99, which is also the volume sweet spot.
- **Four or five** — Pro at $182, and you are paying $996 a year for seats four and five.
- **More than five** — Peace of Mind at $299, which at least adds unlimited documents alongside its 10 seats.

**If your team is four people, look hard at whether the fourth genuinely needs a login**, because that one seat is what moves you from $99 to $182.

## Peace of Mind is the better-value upgrade

This is counterintuitive, so it is worth spelling out.

Going Standard → Pro costs **+$83/month** and buys **2 seats, 0 documents**.
Going Pro → Peace of Mind costs **+$117/month** and buys **5 seats and unlimited documents**.

The second jump is more expensive in absolute terms and considerably better value per thing acquired. If you are already contemplating Pro for seat reasons, the tier above it is worth pricing properly rather than dismissing as the expensive one.

The asterisk on "Unlimited* Documents" is Surfer's own, and its terms are not stated on the pricing page. **Ask what it means before budgeting around it** — an unqualified asterisk is a question, not a feature.

## The AI product sits outside all of it

Surfer lists **AI Search Analytics at $158/month** billed yearly for 100 prompts, as a standalone line rather than an inclusion in the four content plans.

That matters for anyone reading "AI SEO" on the pricing page and assuming the AI search-visibility tracking is part of what they are buying. **It is a separate purchase**, and at $158 it is more than the Standard content plan it would sit next to.

Tracking whether AI assistants cite your content is a genuinely new and genuinely useful category. It is also a second subscription, and the honest way to budget it is as one.

## What the price does not tell you

Content optimisation scores are directional, not authoritative. A tool that reads the pages currently ranking and tells you what they have in common is describing correlation — it cannot tell you which of those things is *why* they rank.

Used well, that is still valuable: it catches the obvious gaps, keeps a writer honest about depth, and turns "write something good about X" into a brief. Used badly, it produces content that hits a target score and reads like it.

**No pricing table can tell you which of those you will do**, and it is the difference between $588 a year well spent and $588 a year on a number that made you feel productive.

## Who should not buy this

**If you publish fewer than about ten pieces a month**, 120 documents is far more than you need and $49 is buying headroom you will not touch. The constraint on a small site is almost never optimisation — it is publishing at all.

**If nobody on the team writes**, this is a tool with no operator. It scores and briefs content; it does not produce it, and a content tool without a writer is a subscription to a to-do list.

## The honest summary

**Discovery at $49 and Standard at $99 are cleanly priced and the step between them is real** — triple the documents for double the money.

**Above that, you are buying seats, and Standard to Pro is $996 a year for two of them with no extra output.** Count the logins you actually need first, and if the answer is four or more, price Peace of Mind properly rather than defaulting to Pro.
