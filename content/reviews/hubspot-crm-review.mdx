## Two numbers decide whether free works for you

HubSpot's free CRM is not a trial. There is no time limit and no card required, and that is unusual enough to say plainly before anything else.

It has two hard limits, and almost everyone checks the wrong one first.

- **1,000 contacts**
- **2 users**

Contacts is the number people look at. **Seats is the number that ends it.**

## Why seats runs out before contacts

Think about the order these actually grow in.

A new business signs its third team member long before it collects its thousandth contact. A founder, a salesperson and one more hire — that is three seats, and free supports two. Meanwhile the contact database might be at 200.

**You get pushed onto a paid plan by hiring, not by succeeding at marketing.** That is a fine business model and HubSpot is not hiding it, but it changes when you should plan for the cost: budget from your headcount forecast, not your list growth.

## The Starter price has two versions

Here is where the arithmetic needs care. HubSpot's pricing page shows Starter at:

| Billing | Per seat, per month |
|---|---|
| **Annually** | **$7** |
| Regular / month-to-month | **$20** |

That is not a small discount. **The month-to-month price is roughly three times the advertised one**, and $7 is the number that appears in the marketing.

Run it for a three-person team:

| | Annual billing | Month-to-month |
|---|---|---|
| 3 seats | $21/mo | $60/mo |
| Over 12 months | **$252** | **$720** |

The annual commitment is the sensible choice *if* you already know this is your CRM. It is a poor choice while you are still deciding, and the decision usually gets made in the first few weeks — exactly when you know least.

**Pay month-to-month until the tool has survived a quarter, then buy the year.** The premium you pay for that certainty is real, and it is smaller than the cost of prepaying for the wrong platform.

## What Starter actually includes

The free-to-Starter step buys less than the price difference suggests. From the pricing page, Starter lists 500 calling minutes, **up to 3 email templates**, **up to 3 canned snippets**, **1 deal pipeline**, and meeting scheduling that still carries HubSpot branding.

Those are small numbers. Three email templates is not a marketing system; it is three email templates. The main thing Starter removes is the seat cap and some of the branding.

**The serious automation is not on Starter.** It lives in the higher Hub tiers, and those are priced per product — Marketing, Sales, Service and Content are separate lines on the pricing page. If you are evaluating HubSpot as a marketing automation platform rather than a contact database, the free CRM tells you almost nothing about what that will cost.

## The branding detail worth checking

On the free tier, HubSpot's own branding appears on customer-facing features including meeting scheduling. If you are sending booking links to clients, that is your prospect seeing another company's logo in your sales process.

Whether that matters depends entirely on who your customers are. For a B2B consultancy it is a real problem. For an internal pipeline nobody outside sees, it is nothing. **Look at which of your free-tier features are customer-facing before deciding it is cosmetic.**

## Where a different category fits better

HubSpot is a CRM first. If what you actually need is to *run client work* — separate workspaces per client, white-labelled, resold under your own name — that is a different product shape. [GoHighLevel's pricing is built around sub-accounts and reselling](/reviews/gohighlevel-review), starting at $97/month with unlimited contacts and unlimited users but only three sub-accounts.

And if you need funnels, checkout and courses rather than a sales pipeline, neither of these is the category. [Systeme.io's free plan runs 2,000 contacts with unlimited emails](/reviews/systeme-io-review), twice HubSpot's free contact ceiling, with funnels and a course platform included.

## The honest summary

**The free CRM is a genuinely good product given away, and the 1,000-contact limit is generous.** Anyone dismissing it as a crippled demo has not used it.

**Plan for the seat cap, not the contact cap.** Two users is the wall, hiring is what hits it, and the $7 seat price that makes the next step look cheap is an annual commitment. Know which of those two prices you are actually comparing before you call HubSpot the cheap option.

*Free-tier limits, seat caps, and both Starter prices are from HubSpot's own pricing page, checked 10 August 2026.*
