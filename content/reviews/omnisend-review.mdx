## Read the contacts column, not the emails column

| | Free | Standard | Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | **$0** | **$11.20** | **$41.30** |
| **Contacts** | **250** | **500** | **2,500** |
| Emails/month | 500 | 6,000 | **Unlimited** |
| Web push | 500 | Unlimited | Unlimited |

Pro's headline is unlimited monthly emails. But it holds **2,500 contacts** — and with 2,500 contacts, unlimited sending means you may email the same 2,500 people as often as you like.

For an ecommerce store the binding resource is almost always **list size**, not send volume. You are trying to grow the number of people you can reach. Unlimited sends to a capped list removes a constraint that was not the problem.

**So the tier decision here is a contact-count decision**, and the emails column is close to noise until your list is large.

## 500 contacts is smaller than it sounds

Standard doubles Free's 250 to 500 for $11.20 a month.

A store with a working popup collects 500 email addresses in weeks, not months. So Standard is realistically a **first-few-months plan** rather than a destination, and the honest question when signing up is not "is $11.20 good value" but "how long until I am on Pro."

At 2,500 contacts, Pro at $41.30 is the real operating tier for a store with any traction. **That is the number to compare against alternatives**, and it is 3.7× the figure that gets quoted.

The page describes the Custom plan as being for "businesses with custom needs, large amount of contacts or email volume" without stating a price — so above 2,500 contacts, pricing becomes a conversation.

## SMS is a separate, published meter

Omnisend publishes global SMS rates that fall with spend:

| Monthly SMS spend | Rate |
|---|---|
| $10 – $49.99 | $0.009 |
| $50 – $999.99 | $0.0085 |
| $1,000 – $9,999.99 | $0.008 |
| $10,000+ | $0.007 |

Pro states SMS "starting at $0.007", which is the top-volume rate — reachable only at $10,000 a month of SMS. **The rate you will actually pay starting out is $0.009**, and quoting the floor is a small piece of optimism worth correcting.

In practice: a single SMS campaign to 2,500 contacts costs **$22.50** at the entry rate. Not large, but it is a per-send cost on top of the subscription, and SMS is the reason many stores choose this platform.

## Web push is the quiet inclusion

Free includes **500 web push notifications**; Standard and Pro are **unlimited**.

Web push rarely appears in comparisons and is genuinely useful for ecommerce — cart abandonment and back-in-stock alerts reach people who never gave you an email address. Unlimited from $11.20 is a real inclusion rather than a token one.

If push is part of your plan, it materially improves Standard's value at a tier whose contact limit otherwise looks thin.

## The prepay discount, stated plainly

The page shows a 3-month prepay option: **$33.60 for Standard** and **$123.90 for Pro**, each marked −30%.

That is a straightforward discount on a short commitment, which is a gentler ask than an annual lock-in. If you know you are staying a quarter, it is money left on the table otherwise.

## Who should not buy this

**If your list is already past 2,500**, the published ladder ends and you are into Custom and a sales conversation — so get that quote before assuming this is the budget option.

**If you do not sell physical products**, much of the value here is ecommerce-specific: cart recovery, back-in-stock, product blocks. A general newsletter does not need it.

## The honest summary

**The free tier is real, web push is unlimited from $11.20, and the SMS rate card is published in full — all better than the category norm.**

**Just size it on contacts.** Free is 250, Standard is 500, Pro is 2,500 with unlimited sends. For any store that is growing a list, Pro at $41.30 is the operating price, and "unlimited emails" is not the constraint being removed.
