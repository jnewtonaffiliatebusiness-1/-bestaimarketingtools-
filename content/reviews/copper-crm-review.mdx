## Two limits, and the small one decides

Copper publishes contact and company allowances separately, and almost everyone reads only the first:

| | Basic | Professional | Business |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | $29/seat | $69/seat | $134/seat |
| **Annual** | **$23/seat** | **$59/seat** | **$99/seat** |
| Contacts | 2,500 | 15,000 | Unlimited |
| **Companies** | **25** | **50** | **Unlimited** |

Look at the bottom row. **Basic allows 25 companies. Professional allows 50.**

In a B2B pipeline a "company" is an account — an organisation you are selling to. Fifty accounts is a small book. A single salesperson working mid-market territory will pass 50 companies inside a quarter, while still nowhere near Professional's 15,000 contacts.

**So the ceiling that ends your plan is almost certainly the company count, not the contact count** — and it is the number nobody quotes, because contacts are what pricing pages lead with.

## Do the ratio

The two allowances imply a contacts-per-company ratio, and it is worth checking against your own reality:

- Basic: 2,500 contacts ÷ 25 companies = **100 contacts per company**
- Professional: 15,000 ÷ 50 = **300 contacts per company**

A hundred contacts at one company is enterprise-account territory. If you sell to small businesses — one or two people per account — you will hold 50 companies and perhaps 100 contacts, having consumed 100% of your company allowance and 4% of your contact allowance.

**That mismatch is the single most useful thing to know before choosing a tier here.** If your accounts are small and numerous, price Business, because the middle tier's ceiling is not where its headline suggests.

## The annual discount is genuinely large at the top

The page advertises "up to 26% off" for annual billing, and unusually the biggest saving is on the most expensive plan:

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | $29 | $23 | 21% |
| Professional | $69 | $59 | 14% |
| **Business** | **$134** | **$99** | **26%** |

Business saves **$35 a seat a month, $420 a year per person.** A five-person team on Business is $5,940 annually rather than $8,040 — a $2,100 difference for committing to a year.

That is the largest absolute saving on the page and it sits on the tier most likely to be bought by a team that already knows it is staying. Worth taking if you are one of them.

## Business is the only tier without ceilings

Unlimited contacts *and* unlimited companies arrive together at $99 a seat annually.

That is a clean design — one tier where you stop counting — but it means the step up is not incremental. You go from 50 companies to no limit, and from $59 to $99 a seat. There is no middle option for a team that needs 200 companies and nothing else.

For a five-person team that is **$200 a month more** to remove a limit they may exceed by a small margin. Annoying, and worth modelling honestly before assuming Professional will hold.

## What the page does not tell you

Three things are **not stated**: minimum seat counts, storage limits, and email-send limits.

The seat minimum matters most. Some CRMs in this bracket require three or five seats, which changes a solo operator's real entry price from $23 to $69 or $115 a month. **Ask before assuming you can buy one seat.**

Storage and send limits are the sort of thing that surfaces later, and their absence from the pricing page means they cannot be compared from the outside.

## Who should not buy this

**If you are not on Google Workspace**, the main architectural reason to choose Copper is its Gmail-native design. Outside that ecosystem you are comparing it on general CRM merits against a wide field.

**If you sell to many small accounts**, read the company limits above again before starting on Basic or Professional. The tier you need is probably Business, and the honest comparison price is $99 a seat, not $23.

## The honest summary

**The pricing is transparent, the annual discount is real, and $23 a seat is a genuinely low entry.**

**But check the company limit, not the contact limit.** 25 companies on Basic and 50 on Professional are the numbers that will actually stop you, and for most B2B teams they arrive first — which makes Business at $99 a seat the tier to price against alternatives.
