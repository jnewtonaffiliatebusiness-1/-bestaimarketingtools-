## The pricing page has one number on it

Not a comparison grid. Not three columns with the middle one highlighted. **$15 per user per month, plus applicable tax.**

In a category where the standard move is to publish a cheap headline tier that omits the one feature everyone needs, this is worth pausing on. The vendor states it directly:

> No tiers. No contracts. No features locked behind upgrades.

For anyone who has priced a CRM recently, the significance is not the $15. It is that the $15 is the whole answer, and you can compare it to a competitor without first working out which tier is the honest comparison.

## What is included at the only price there is

Everything the product does:

- Unlimited contacts and companies
- Unlimited pipelines
- Unlimited custom fields
- 25GB file storage **per user**
- Task management, email logging, mobile access
- User permissions
- All features and free upgrades
- Free support

The storage detail is quietly good design. Most vendors cap storage per account, so adding people makes the shared pool tighter. Here a five-person team has 125GB rather than a fixed allocation to fight over — **the resource grows with the thing that consumes it.**

## The cost is still per person

Flat pricing is not the same as cheap pricing, and the arithmetic is unchanged from any per-seat tool:

| Team size | Per month | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15 | $180 |
| 3 | $45 | $540 |
| 5 | $75 | $900 |
| 10 | **$150** | **$1,800** |

What the flat rate removes is the *cliff* — the moment a tiered competitor tells you that the feature you now need lives two tiers up, and your bill triples for one checkbox. That cliff is the single most common CRM budgeting failure, and it does not exist here.

**What it does not remove is the slope.** Ten people still cost ten times one person.

## The trade you are actually making

Here is the part that a positive review usually skips.

"No features locked behind upgrades" is a genuine promise in both directions. There is nothing withheld — and there is also nothing above you. When you need something the product does not do, there is no plan to move to. **The answer is not "upgrade", it is "use a different tool."**

That reframes the decision. You are not choosing a starting tier and preserving the option to grow inside it. You are choosing a product whose scope is fixed, and betting your requirements will stay inside it.

For a solo consultant or a three-person team tracking relationships and follow-ups, that bet is close to free — those needs are stable for years. For a team that expects to add lifecycle marketing, revenue reporting or multi-stage automation within eighteen months, the bet is real, and the cost of losing it is a migration rather than an upgrade.

## Where the boundary sits

Concretely, the things that will send you elsewhere: marketing automation, email campaign sending, and sophisticated reporting. This is a contact and pipeline tool. It records what happened and what is next; it does not run campaigns at your list or model your funnel.

Pairing it with a separate email platform is the normal workaround and it works fine — but it means two subscriptions and two places where contact data lives, which is exactly the fragmentation an all-in-one is sold to prevent. **Count that second tool in the price comparison, or you are comparing $15 against a number that includes it.**

## Thirty days, no card

The trial is 30 days with no credit card and cancel-anytime. That is longer than the 14 days that has become standard, and the missing card requirement matters more than it sounds — it removes the forgot-to-cancel revenue that a lot of trials quietly depend on.

Thirty days is also, usefully, long enough to find the boundary. **Spend the trial deliberately trying to do the most complex thing you will realistically need**, rather than the easy thing. If it holds, the fixed scope is not a risk. If it does not, you found out for nothing.

## Who should not buy this

**If you are evaluating CRMs to fix a process problem**, no CRM fixes that, and a simple one fixes it least. This tool assumes you know your follow-up process and want somewhere to keep it. It will not impose one.

**If a decision-maker wants dashboards**, the reporting here is deliberately basic. That is a design choice rather than a gap, and it will not change with a bigger plan, because there is no bigger plan.

## The honest summary

**The pricing is exactly what it claims and the model is more honest than the category norm.** One number, everything included, thirty days to check, no contract.

**The question is not whether $15 is good value — it is.** It is whether a product with a deliberately fixed ceiling matches where your team will be in two years, because the flat price that removes the upgrade trap also removes the upgrade.
