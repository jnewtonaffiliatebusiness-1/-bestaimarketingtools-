## The number, and whether it is real

Kit — the company formerly called ConvertKit — offers a free plan that carries **up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited email broadcasts.**

From Kit's own pricing page, checked August 2026:

| Plan | Price | Subscribers |
|---|---|---|
| **Newsletter** | **$0** | **up to 10,000** |
| Creator | $33/mo | from 1,000 |
| Pro | $66/mo | from 1,000 |

That free number is not a typo and it is not a trial. **For comparison, [Mailchimp's free plan is 250 contacts](/reviews/mailchimp-review)** — Kit's is forty times larger.

So the reasonable reaction is suspicion. Something has to be missing.

## What is actually missing

It is not capacity. It is **automation**.

The free Newsletter plan sends **broadcasts** — one email, written now, sent to a list or a segment. That is a newsletter, and if a newsletter is what you publish, the free plan does the whole job at any size up to 10,000 people.

What it does not do is **sequences**: the automated welcome series that fires when someone subscribes, the multi-step launch sequence, the behaviour-triggered follow-up. Those live on Creator at $33/month.

**That is a coherent product decision rather than a trap.** Kit is betting that writers start by publishing and only later need automation, and that by the time they do they will have an audience worth paying to automate against.

## Why the paid entry price looks high

Creator at **$33/month for 1,000 subscribers** is expensive next to entry tiers elsewhere — [MailerLite's Comfort starts at $12](/reviews/mailerlite-review), [Mailchimp's Essentials at $13](/reviews/mailchimp-review).

Two things make the comparison less unfavourable than it looks.

**You were free until you needed automation.** A creator who spends a year building to 4,000 subscribers on the free plan has paid nothing during the period when every other tool would have been charging. The lifetime cost is not the monthly price.

**You are not paying for subscriber headroom.** On most platforms the entry tier is a volume tier and you climb it as your list grows. On Kit the entry tier is a capability tier, and you crossed into it because you wanted sequences, not because you hit a ceiling.

## Who should use the free plan and stay there

**Anyone publishing a newsletter on a schedule.** If your relationship with your list is "I write, you read", broadcasts are the entire feature set you need and 10,000 subscribers is a long runway.

**Anyone validating an audience before committing.** You can find out whether people want to hear from you without a card on file, and without the artificial urgency of a 14-day trial.

## Who will hit the wall early

**Anyone selling something.** A welcome sequence is not a nice-to-have when you have an offer — it is the mechanism. You will want Creator in month one, and the free plan's generosity is irrelevant to you.

**Anyone who needs funnels and checkout as well as email.** Kit is a dedicated email tool that has added commerce features; it is not an all-in-one. [Systeme.io's free plan is smaller on contacts at 2,000](/reviews/systeme-io-free-plan-limits) but includes sales funnels, a course platform and affiliate management — a different shape of product for a different job. If you are choosing between them, the question is not which free tier is bigger. It is whether you are publishing or selling.

## The honest summary

**Kit's free plan is the real thing and the most generous in the category.** The catch is narrow and clearly signposted: you get reach for free and pay for automation.

If you write to an audience, start here and pay nothing for a long time. **If you sell to one, price Creator from day one** and treat the free tier as a trial you happen not to be paying for.
