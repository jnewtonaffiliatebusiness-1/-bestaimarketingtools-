## The number most people are working from is out of date

Ask anyone to recommend a free email tool and Mailchimp comes up first. That recommendation is usually inherited from an article written when Mailchimp's free plan carried **2,000 contacts**.

It does not any more. From Mailchimp's own pricing page, checked August 2026:

| Free plan | |
|---|---|
| **Contacts** | **250** |
| **Monthly sends** | **500** |
| Price | $0 |

**Five hundred sends a month is one email to 250 people, twice.** If you email weekly, you are over the limit in the third week of every month.

That is not a criticism of Mailchimp for changing its pricing — every company does. It is a criticism of the advice ecosystem that still points beginners there on the strength of a number that no longer exists.

## What the paid tiers cost

| Plan | Price | Contacts | Monthly sends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 250 | 500 |
| **Essentials** | **$13/mo** | 500 | 5,000 |
| **Standard** | **$20/mo** | 500 | 6,000 |
| **Premium** | **$350/mo** | 10,000 | 150,000 |

Two things in that table are worth pausing on.

**Essentials to Standard is $7 for 1,000 extra sends.** The contact limit does not move. You are buying features, not headroom.

**Standard to Premium is a $330 jump.** There is no tier between $20 and $350. If you outgrow Standard's limits before you need Premium's feature set, you pay for the feature set anyway or you go up the contact tiers within Standard and watch the price climb.

## The overage clause is the part to read

Mailchimp's pricing page states plainly that **overages apply if you exceed the contact or the email send limit.**

That matters more than it sounds. Most email tools handle a limit by refusing to send or prompting an upgrade — a hard stop you notice. An overage is a soft stop you discover on the invoice.

**Before you commit, find your actual monthly send volume**, not your list size. Contacts × emails per month is the number that decides whether you are on the right plan, and it is the number the pricing page does not calculate for you.

## Where Mailchimp is still the right answer

**If you have never sent a marketing email before**, Mailchimp's interface is the gentlest on-ramp available and that has real value. Fewer people abandon it in week one than abandon more capable tools.

**If your stack is already built around it**, the integration library is the widest in the category and switching costs are real.

**If you need one specific integration** that only Mailchimp supports, that decides it and no price comparison matters.

## Where it is not

**If you are choosing on the free tier**, Mailchimp is now the most restrictive of the major options. [Kit's free plan carries 10,000 subscribers](/reviews/convertkit-review) and [MailerLite's sends 2,500 emails a month at the same 250 subscribers](/reviews/mailerlite-review) — five times Mailchimp's allowance for the same audience size.

**If you want funnels and courses as well as email**, this is the wrong category of tool entirely. [Systeme.io's free plan runs 2,000 contacts with unlimited emails](/reviews/systeme-io-free-plan-limits) and adds sales funnels and a course platform, because it is an all-in-one rather than a dedicated email sender. It is worse at email design than Mailchimp and better at everything around it.

## The honest summary

Mailchimp is a competent email platform with an outdated reputation for generosity. **At $13 and above it is fine and unremarkable. On the free tier it is now the weakest option in its own category**, and the only reason it is still recommended there is that the internet has not caught up.

Work out your monthly send volume first. If it is over 500, the free plan was never a real option and the comparison starts at $13 — where several tools give you more.

*All figures from Mailchimp's own pricing page, checked August 2026. Plans and limits change; verify before you buy.*
