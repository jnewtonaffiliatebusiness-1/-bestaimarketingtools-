## Count profiles, not brands

The pricing page is clear about what it meters, and it is worth reading literally:

| | Bootstrap | Accelerate | Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | **$29** | **$49** | **$99** |
| Annual | $290/yr | $490/yr | $990/yr |
| Social profiles | **5** | **10** | **25** |
| Users per workspace | 1 | 1 | **3** |
| Workspaces | 1 | 1 | 5 |

Now count what one ordinary business actually runs: Facebook page, Instagram, LinkedIn company page, X, TikTok, Pinterest. **That is six profiles for one brand** — past Bootstrap before a single post is scheduled.

Add a founder's personal LinkedIn, which for most B2B businesses outperforms the company page, and it is seven. Add YouTube and it is eight.

**So the honest entry price for a business with a normal social footprint is Accelerate at $49, not the $29 on the poster.** Bootstrap fits a business deliberately running three or four channels — which is a legitimate and often smarter strategy, but it is a choice, not the default.

## The single-user ceiling arrives before the profile ceiling

Here is the constraint most teams hit first, and it is not the one they shopped for.

**Bootstrap and Accelerate both include 1 user per workspace.** Team access begins at **Pro, $99/month**, where it goes to 3.

That means a two-person setup — say a founder who approves and a VA who schedules — is a $99/month product, even if between them they only run six profiles that would fit comfortably in the $49 tier.

The workaround everyone reaches for is sharing one login. It works, and it costs you the audit trail: with a shared seat you cannot tell who scheduled the post that went out wrong, and you cannot remove one person's access when they leave without changing the password for everybody. **Decide that consciously rather than drifting into it**, particularly if a client's accounts are connected.

## Where the agency tiers make sense

The agency ladder is published rather than hidden, which is worth crediting:

| | Pro50 | Pro100 | Pro150 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | $179 | $329 | $449 |
| Profiles | 50 | 100 | 150 |
| Users per workspace | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Workspaces | 10 | 20 | 30 |

Per profile, this gets cheaper as it scales — **$3.58 a profile at Pro50, $2.99 at Pro150** — which is the right shape for volume pricing.

The number to watch is workspaces rather than profiles. Ten workspaces at Pro50 means ten clients kept separate, and at six profiles each you would consume 60 profiles against a 50-profile allowance. **The two limits do not run out together**, and whichever binds first decides your tier. Work out both.

## Unlimited AI, and what that is worth

All plans state unlimited AI content generation with no credit meter. In a year when most tools are moving the opposite direction — bundling AI in and metering it — that is a genuine differentiator and worth naming.

It is also worth being level-headed about what unlimited AI social copy is for. It removes the blank page, which is the part most people find hardest and skip posting over. It does not know your customers, and volume without judgement is how a feed ends up sounding like every other feed in the category.

**Treat it as a first-draft engine, not a content strategy**, and the unlimited part becomes an advantage rather than an invitation.

## The number the page does not give you

Posting limits per plan are **not stated** on the pricing page. For a scheduling tool that is a real omission — how many posts a month a tier permits is not a detail, it is the product.

If you are moving from a tool that capped you, or you run high-frequency channels, **get that number confirmed before subscribing.** It is the one figure here that could change the answer and cannot be checked from the outside.

## Who should not buy this

**If you post to one or two networks**, this is more scheduling infrastructure than the job needs, and both platforms' native schedulers are free.

**If your problem is that nobody engages**, scheduling more efficiently will not fix it. A scheduler makes publishing cheaper; it does not make anyone care, and posting six times a week into silence costs $49 a month more than posting twice.

## The honest summary

**The pricing is transparent and the per-profile logic is consistent all the way up the ladder.** Annual billing saves about 16% and the agency tiers are properly published.

**Just price it on your real profile count and your real user count, not the headline.** Six networks and two people is Pro at $99/month — not the $29 that gets quoted — and knowing that before you sign up is the difference between a tool that fits and a tier you outgrow in week one.
