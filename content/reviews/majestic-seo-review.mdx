## Three prices, and then seven variables

The headline is simple enough:

| | LITE | PRO | API |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | **$49.99** | **$99.99** | **$399.99** |
| Annual | $499.99/yr | $999.99/yr | $3,999.90/yr |
| Monthly equivalent on annual | $41.67 | $83.33 | $333.33 |

Annual billing saves about 17% on every tier — two months free, in effect, which is standard and fair.

Then the plans differentiate on seven separate allowances at once, and this is where the tool becomes hard to buy.

| Allowance | LITE | PRO | API |
|---|---|---|---|
| Users | **1** | **1** | **5** |
| Analysis units | 1 million | 20 million | 100 million |
| Retrieval units | 200,000 | 5 million | 20 million |
| Index item units | 5,000 | 125,000 | 500,000 |
| Site Explorer data rows | 5,000 | 30,000 | 50,000 |
| Analysis depth | 5,000 | 30,000 | 50,000 |
| Report units | 60 | 300 | 950 |

## The estimation problem

Ask yourself how many analysis units a competitor backlink audit consumes.

You cannot answer it. Neither can anyone who has not already run one on this specific tool, because the unit is internal to Majestic and has no equivalent anywhere else. The same goes for retrieval units, index item units and report units — four distinct currencies, each metering a different action, none of them convertible to "how many sites can I look at this month."

**This is the real cost of the pricing model, and it is not the dollar figure.** With a seat-priced tool you know what you are buying before you pay. Here the honest answer to "which plan do I need" is *start on Lite and find out*, which means the first month is a paid experiment in reading your own usage meters.

To be fair to Majestic: the allowances are all published, per plan, on the public pricing page. Plenty of competitors put this behind a demo call. The information is there — it just is not information you can act on in advance.

## Pro is not twice Lite, it is twenty times

Look at the multipliers rather than the prices.

Doubling the spend from $49.99 to $99.99 buys **20× the analysis units** (1m → 20m), **25× the retrieval units** (200k → 5m), **25× the index item units** (5,000 → 125,000), and **6× the data rows and analysis depth**.

That is a very steep curve, and it tells you something about how the tiers are meant to be read: **Lite is not a small version of Pro, it is a sampler.** A 20:1 jump for a 2:1 price is not how a vendor prices two plans it expects the same person to choose between. It is how a vendor prices a taster and a working tool.

If you are doing backlink analysis as a regular part of the job rather than occasionally, the pricing structure is quietly telling you Lite will not hold.

## The seat count is the sharp edge

**Lite includes 1 user. Pro also includes 1 user.** Five users appear only on API at $399.99 a month.

So a three-person SEO team has no middle option. Pro's allowances are ample for three people's work, but the licence covers one login, and the next plan up costs four times as much and is built for programmatic access rather than more humans at a dashboard.

The pricing page does not state the cost of adding a seat to Lite or Pro. That is the single most consequential missing number on the page for any team — **ask before you buy, because the published ladder has no rung between one person and $399.99.**

## Analysis depth is the one to check first

Of the seven allowances, the one most likely to stop you mid-task is **analysis depth: 5,000 on Lite**.

Link analysis on a large or well-established domain reaches into hundreds of thousands of referring URLs. A 5,000 ceiling means you are looking at a slice, and the slice is chosen by the tool, not by you. For a small local competitor that is the whole picture; for a national publisher it is a sample you may mistake for a total.

**A partial dataset that looks complete is worse than an obvious limit**, because nothing in the interface tells you the answer would have changed with more depth. If you are analysing large sites, this is the number that decides your plan, ahead of price.

## What it is not

This is a backlink and link-intelligence tool. It is not a keyword research suite, a rank tracker or a content platform, and it does not pretend to be.

An earlier version of this review asserted that its data was less fresh than a named competitor's. **That claim was never sourced and has been removed** — index freshness comparisons require running both indexes against a known set of new links, which is a real test nobody here has run. What is on the record is the allowance structure above, and that is enough to decide by.

## Who should not buy this

**If you need one tool for the whole job**, this is not it, and the $49.99 will sit on top of another subscription rather than replacing anything.

**If you are not sure why you need backlink data**, start with what you already have — Search Console reports links to your site for free. Majestic answers questions about *other people's* links, which is competitive research, and that is a different activity from improving your own site.

## The honest summary

**The index and the link metrics are why people pay for this, and Lite at $49.99 is a genuinely low entry price for dedicated link data.**

**But you cannot size your plan in advance, and there is no seat option between one user and $399.99 a month.** Budget for a first month spent learning what your own usage looks like, check the analysis-depth ceiling against the size of sites you actually study, and get a per-seat price in writing before putting a team on it.

*All plan prices, annual equivalents, user counts and the seven per-plan allowances are from Majestic's own plans-and-pricing page, checked 10 August 2026. The cost of additional seats is not stated on that page.*
