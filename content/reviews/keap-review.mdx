## The published price and the four things it does not settle

Keap does something most of its competitors do not: it offers **one plan**, not a tier ladder. The whole platform, one price.

That price is **$299/month, billed at $2,988/year**.

Then look at what you still cannot work out from the pricing page:

| Line on your invoice | Published? |
|---|---|
| Base platform | ✅ **$299/mo** |
| Extra user licences | ✅ **$39/mo each** (2 included) |
| Contacts above the included allowance | ❌ *"tiers"* — no figures shown |
| Required implementation package | ❌ **no price at all** |

**Two of four are visible.** For a product whose main promise is simplicity, that is the thing to weigh.

## The user count is smaller than it sounds

$299/month includes **2 user licences**. Additional users are **$39/month each**.

For a service business — the market Keap explicitly targets — two seats is an owner and one other person. A five-person operation is:

```
$299  base (2 users)
$117  3 extra users x $39
----
$416/month  =  $4,992/year
```

Before contacts. Before implementation.

**Count your seats before you compare Keap to anything else.** The $299 headline and a five-person team are $117/month apart, and per-seat pricing is the line that quietly scales with your success.

## The contact allowance you have to ask for

Keap's pricing page does not state how many contacts $299 includes. It says additional contact pricing is **tiered, with the cost per contact falling as you add larger quantities** — and directs you to a calculator or a chat.

Volume discounting is normal and reasonable. The issue is sequencing: **you cannot compare Keap's total cost to a competitor's without starting a sales conversation**, while nearly every alternative publishes a contacts-to-price table you can read in thirty seconds.

That is a deliberate choice by Keap, and it is a real cost to you in time and in negotiating position. Go in with your contact count already written down.

## The required implementation package

This is the part most comparisons miss entirely. Keap states that **implementation services are required**, and its FAQ explains the reasoning: after twenty years of helping small businesses implement software, the ones that succeed are those with strategy, support and services.

That reasoning is honest and, in fairness, probably correct — complex automation platforms do fail from bad setup more than bad software.

But **no price for it is published**, and it is not optional. So the true first-year cost of Keap is:

```
$2,988   annual platform (2 users)
+ $468/yr per extra user
+ contacts, tiered, unpublished
+ mandatory implementation, unpublished
```

**Get the implementation quote in writing before you sign anything.** It is the largest unknown in the arithmetic and the only one with no published anchor at all.

## Where the money is actually going

Keap is not overpriced for what it is. It is a genuine all-in-one for service businesses — CRM, email, automation and payments in one system — and it has been doing that for two decades. The text marketing tier at the base plan includes 500 messages and 100 voice minutes, with upgrades from $24/month.

The question is whether you need a platform that assumes it must be professionally implemented. **If your automation is a handful of follow-up sequences, you are buying a category above your problem.**

## What to compare it against

If the appeal is one system for a service business, [GoHighLevel starts at $97/month with unlimited contacts and unlimited users](/reviews/gohighlevel-review) — the inverse trade to Keap's, since its limit is sub-accounts rather than seats, and its telecom and AI usage bills on top.

If the appeal is simply selling something without stitching five tools together, [Systeme.io's free plan carries 2,000 contacts, 3 sales funnels, a course and an affiliate program](/reviews/systeme-io-review), with a paid entry at $17/month. That is a smaller product than Keap and it is honest about being one — but the gap between $0 and $299 buys a lot of patience while you find out what you actually need.

## The honest summary

**Keap's single-plan structure is a real virtue and its target market is well chosen.** Service businesses that need CRM, email and payments in one place are underserved, and this is a serious product for them.

**The pricing is where it loses.** $299 covers two users; the third costs $39; the contact allowance is unpublished; and a required implementation package has no price on it anywhere. **You cannot know what Keap costs without a sales call, and that is the finding.** If a competitor lets you work out your total in a minute from a public page, weigh that as a feature.

*Base price, annual figure, included user licences, additional user cost, tiered contact pricing, text marketing tier and the required implementation statement are all from Keap's own pricing page and FAQ, checked 10 August 2026.*
