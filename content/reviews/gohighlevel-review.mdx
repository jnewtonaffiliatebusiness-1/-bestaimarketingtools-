## Start with the number that actually binds

GoHighLevel's pricing page lists **$97/month** for Starter, and the two figures people quote from it are *unlimited contacts* and *unlimited users*. Both are real and both are on the entry plan.

Neither is the limit you will hit.

| Starter — $97/mo | |
|---|---|
| Contacts | **Unlimited** |
| Users | **Unlimited** |
| **Sub-accounts** | **3** |
| Support | 24/7 |

**A sub-account is a client.** Three of them is the ceiling on the $97 plan, and it is the only number on that tier that counts down.

## The step is $200, and it happens at client four

There is no incremental sub-account pricing. When you sign the fourth client, the move is from Starter to **Unlimited at $297/month** — the tier that lists unlimited sub-accounts.

| Plan | Price | Sub-accounts | The thing you are actually buying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $97/mo | 3 | Contacts and users, uncapped |
| **Unlimited** | **$297/mo** | **Unlimited** | Rebill phone & email at no markup, basic API |
| **Agency Pro** | **$497/mo** | Unlimited | **SaaS Mode**, automated sub-account creation, rebill *with* markup, advanced API |
| Enterprise | Custom | — | White-label mobile app, HIPAA |

**Price that jump before you take client four, not after.** Three clients at $97 is $32 a client. Four clients at $297 is $74 a client — and it stays there until you have enough clients to average it back down.

## SaaS Mode is the pitch, and it is on the third tier

Most people arrive at GoHighLevel through the reselling argument: run it white-labelled, charge clients your own price, keep the margin. The feature that automates that is **SaaS Mode**, and the pricing page lists it under **Agency Pro at $497/month** — not Starter, not Unlimited.

That matters because the reselling case is usually made with $97 in the same sentence. The two numbers belong to different plans.

**Rebilling splits the same way.** Unlimited at $297 lets you pass phone and email costs to clients at cost. Agency Pro at $497 lets you pass them on *with a markup*. If reselling usage is part of your margin, the $497 tier is where that begins.

## The costs that are not in any of those numbers

The pricing page says it plainly: usage-based costs for telecommunications and AI services apply on top. Named on the page are **phone numbers, SMS, emails sent, and premium AI features**.

So the monthly bill is:

```
plan price  +  phone numbers  +  SMS  +  email volume  +  AI usage
```

None of that is hidden — it is stated by the vendor — but it is not in the $97, and a platform sold as "everything in one place" invites you to forget it. **Budget the usage line separately from the plan line.** On higher tiers you can rebill it; on Starter you absorb it.

A recent change makes the shape of this clear. In August 2026 GoHighLevel added the option for resellers to charge clients a *fixed* rate for AI products rather than a markup on usage. Good for the client's predictability — and it moves the variance onto you.

## Where breadth costs you depth

The honest case against an all-in-one is not that the parts are missing. It is that any given part can be thinner than the standalone tool it replaced.

A concrete example, from the vendor's own changelog: **until 10 August 2026, a Google Ads campaign built in GoHighLevel was capped at 40 keywords.** That cap is now gone and keywords can be bulk-pasted. But it existed, inside a platform marketed as the place you run everything.

Assume that pattern rather than being surprised by it. **Before you consolidate, list the two or three tools you would actually be retiring and check those specific functions.** The rest of the suite being present is not the same as it being sufficient.

## Who this is genuinely for

**It fits** if you manage marketing for multiple clients, want them separated into sub-accounts, and intend to resell the platform under your own brand. That is the shape the pricing is built around, and nothing else on the market packages it this way at this price.

**It does not fit** if you are one business with one brand. You would be paying $97/month for an agency structure you never use, and the unlimited contacts and users — the headline benefits — are exactly what a single operator does not need.

If that is you, a funnel-and-email platform is the right category instead. [Systeme.io's free plan carries 2,000 contacts, 3 sales funnels, a course and an affiliate program](/reviews/systeme-io-review) with no card and no expiry, and its paid entry is $17/month.

## The honest summary

**GoHighLevel is priced for agencies and it is a fair deal for agencies.** Unlimited contacts and users at $97 is genuinely generous, and no competitor bundles CRM, funnels, email, SMS and white-label reselling this way.

**The $97 is not the number to plan on.** Three sub-accounts is the entry ceiling, unlimited sub-accounts is $297, the reselling engine is $497, and telecom and AI usage sits on top of whichever you pick. Work out which tier your client count puts you on, add a usage estimate, and compare *that* figure to what you are paying now.

*Plan names, prices, sub-account limits and the usage-based charges above are from GoHighLevel's own pricing page; the keyword cap and AI pricing changes are from its public changelog. All checked 10 August 2026.*
