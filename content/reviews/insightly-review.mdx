## The number that is not in the CRM column

Insightly's CRM pricing looks ordinary:

| CRM plan | Per user / month | Records | Storage | Mass email |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plus | **$29** | 100,000 | 10 GB | 2,500/day |
| Professional | **$49** | 250,000 | 100 GB | 5,000/day |
| Enterprise | **$99** | 500,000 | 250 GB | 10,000/day |

Then you reach AppConnect, the integration product, and the pricing page says this:

> **Required technical setup: $3,000**

That fee appears on **all three AppConnect tiers** — Standard at $249/month, Professional at $799, Enterprise at $1,899. It is a one-time charge on top of the subscription, and it is stated publicly rather than surfaced late in a sales process.

**Credit where it is due: publishing it is unusually honest.** Most vendors put implementation fees behind a call. But it means the real first-year cost of the cheapest AppConnect tier is **$249 × 12 + $3,000 = $5,988**, not $2,988 — the setup fee is more than the entire first year of subscription.

## This is four products, not one

The most common mistake with Insightly is pricing the CRM and assuming that is the bill. The pricing page lists four independently priced lines:

| Product | Priced per | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| CRM | user | $29/user/mo |
| **Marketing** | **account** | **$99/mo** |
| Service | user | $29/user/mo |
| **AppConnect** | **account** | **$249/mo + $3,000 setup** |

The per-account versus per-user distinction matters in both directions. Marketing at $99/month for 2,000 prospects does not multiply by headcount, which is genuinely good for a large team. Service at $29 per user does.

A five-person team wanting CRM Professional, Marketing Plus and Service Professional is: (5 × $49) + $99 + (5 × $49) = **$589/month** — before AppConnect, and before its setup fee.

## The bundles are the honest comparison

Insightly publishes all-in-one bundles, and they are the number to quote:

- **Plus — $349/month** (CRM Plus + Marketing Plus + Service Plus + AppConnect 25k)
- **Professional — $899/month**
- **Enterprise — $2,599/month**

Against the five-person à-la-carte figure above, the $349 bundle includes AppConnect 25k — a $249/month line on its own. **For any team actually using more than the CRM, the bundle is where the arithmetic lands**, and comparing Insightly's "$29" against a rival's all-in price is comparing one quarter of this product against all of that one.

The pricing page does not state whether the $3,000 AppConnect setup applies within the bundles. **Ask.** It is a $3,000 question.

## Marketing is priced on prospects, and the send limit is a multiplier

The Marketing tiers scale on prospects with an unusual send formula:

| Marketing | Per month | Prospects | Monthly sends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plus | $99 | 2,000 | **5× prospects** |
| Professional | $499 | 10,000 | **10× prospects** |
| Enterprise | $999 | 20,000 | **15× prospects** |

The multiplier rises with the tier, so Professional's 10,000 prospects carry 100,000 sends while Plus's 2,000 carry 10,000. That is a sensible design — bigger lists get emailed more often per contact — but it means **send capacity is not proportional to price**, and the jump from $99 to $499 buys 5× the prospects and 10× the sends.

## Everything on the page is the annual rate

The pricing page states all prices are USD **billed annually**. Monthly-billing rates are not stated.

So $29 per user is $348 a year committed up front per person, and a five-seat CRM Professional is **$2,940 for the year**. That is not a criticism — annual-only is common — but it removes the option to trial a team for a month, and it should be treated as a year's commitment rather than a monthly cost.

## Who should not buy this

**If you only need a CRM**, you are buying into a four-product suite priced as such, and simpler per-seat CRMs cover contact and pipeline management without the surrounding catalogue.

**If nobody will own the integration work**, do not buy AppConnect. A $3,000 required setup exists precisely because the product assumes technical implementation — and an integration platform nobody configures is the most expensive shelf-ware in this list.

## The honest summary

**The CRM is fairly priced at $29 to $99 per user and the record limits are generous.** Publishing the $3,000 setup fee openly is more transparent than the category norm.

**But price the stack.** Four products, three of them not the CRM, all annual-billed, with a $3,000 one-time fee on the integration tier. The bundles at $349, $899 and $2,599 are the number worth comparing — and confirm whether the setup fee applies to them before signing.
