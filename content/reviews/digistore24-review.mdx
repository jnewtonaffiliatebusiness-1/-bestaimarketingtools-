## The cookie is the reason to be here

Most affiliate programmes give you 30 days. [ClickBank](/reviews/clickbank-review) gives 60, which is already generous. Digistore24 gives **180**.

That is not a marginal difference. On a considered purchase — a course, a piece of software someone has to justify to a business partner — the gap between a click and a decision is routinely measured in weeks. A 30-day cookie quietly hands that sale to whoever the buyer happened to see last. Six months covers almost any realistic deliberation.

If you write content that ranks and keeps earning, rather than running paid traffic to an immediate decision, the cookie window is the single most important number on this page, and Digistore24 wins it outright.

## What it charges

From Digistore24's own published figures, checked August 2026:

| Charge | Amount |
|---|---|
| Transaction fee | **7.9% + $1** |
| Monthly fee | **$0** |
| Setup cost | **$0** |
| Minimum sales requirement | **None** |
| Security reserve | **10% of each sale, held 60 days** |

Set against ClickBank's 7.5% + $1, the headline rate is effectively identical — four tenths of a percent apart. Anyone choosing between the two on transaction cost is optimising the wrong variable. The differences that matter are the cookie, the payout cadence, and what each platform does with your money before it reaches you.

## "Paid three times a week" is true and incomplete

Digistore24 advertises payouts up to **three times per week**. That is genuinely unusual — most networks run fortnightly, and it is the platform's strongest operational selling point.

What the cadence does not tell you is how much of a given sale is eligible to be paid at all. Commissions release in two pieces:

> 90% of the commission is paid after 14 days, and the remaining 10% is paid after 60 days.

So a sale made today does not become money three days from now. **Ninety per cent of it becomes payable in two weeks, and the final tenth in two months.** The three-times-weekly cadence governs how often the already-released balance is swept out — not how fast a sale matures.

That is a reasonable design. Digistore24 is the merchant of record and carries the refund liability, so holding a slice against returns is the same logic behind ClickBank's reserve. The point is only that the fast-payout headline and the actual cash timeline are answering different questions, and affiliates forecasting from the first one will be short by a tenth for their first two months.

## The threshold question — check this in your own account

Digistore24's marketing states affiliates are paid "with no minimum threshold." Its help centre is widely reported to state minimums for payout instead — figures around €50 for a first payout and €200 thereafter circulate in affiliate write-ups.

**We could not open the help centre page to verify those figures**, so we are not printing them as fact. What we can say is that the two sources are described as saying different things, and a payout minimum is exactly the kind of term that decides whether a beginner sees any money in month one.

Log in and read your own account's payout settings before you plan around "no minimum." If your account shows a threshold, that is the number that governs you, whatever the blog says.

## The trap that costs the most money

This one is not a fee and does not appear on any pricing page.

**Some vendors list a product on Digistore24 whose commission covers the front-end purchase only.** The upsells that follow — the ones carrying most of the funnel's value — pay nothing, or pay only to the vendor. The same product sold through another network may pay across the entire funnel.

The marketplace listing does not put this in front of you. You have to read the vendor's own affiliate terms and compare the commission structure against what the funnel actually sells.

The practical consequence: a product that looks like it pays 60% may pay 60% of the cheapest thing in the funnel. Before you promote anything here, find out **what proportion of the funnel's total value your commission actually touches** — and if the same product is listed on more than one network, compare the two structures before choosing where to send traffic. They are frequently not the same deal.

## Digistore24 vs ClickBank, briefly

| | Digistore24 | ClickBank |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction fee | 7.9% + $1 | 7.5% + $1 |
| Cookie window | **180 days** | 60 days |
| Payout cadence | Up to 3× weekly | Per pay period |
| Reserve | 10%, 60 days | 10%, ~84 days |
| Payout gate | Threshold (verify in-account) | 5 sales across 2 payment methods |

The honest summary is that Digistore24 is the better platform for a content affiliate — the long cookie and quick cadence both favour traffic that converts slowly — while ClickBank's marketplace transparency is stronger before you commit. Neither is meaningfully cheaper than the other.

## The honest read

Digistore24 does the important things well. The cookie is best in class, the fee is ordinary, the payout rhythm is fast, and being merchant of record removes a genuine administrative burden if you sell into the EU.

The caution is not about the platform's cost. It is that two of its most attractive claims — fast payouts and no minimum threshold — are less absolute than they sound, and that the marketplace will happily let you promote a funnel you only get paid on one slice of. Both are knowable in advance. Neither is volunteered.
