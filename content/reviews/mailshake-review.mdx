## The sentence that sets your bill

Buried in the pricing structure is the line that decides what this actually costs:

> Billing is based on how many users you purchase. **Each user receives one mail account connection.**

For most software, seats equal people. For cold email, that is not how the work is done.

Deliverability practice is to spread sending across **several addresses and often several domains**, so no single mailbox carries enough volume to get flagged. A one-person operation running four sending addresses is a normal, careful setup — and on this pricing model it is **four users**.

| Sending addresses | Email Outreach cost/month |
|---|---|
| 1 | $49 |
| 2 | $98 |
| **4** | **$196** |
| 8 | $392 |

**One person, four mailboxes, is $196 a month at the middle tier** — not $49. That is the number to compare, and it does not appear anywhere on the pricing page because the page prices users, not mailboxes.

## The tiers themselves are straightforward

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Sends/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | $29/user | $25/user | **1,500** |
| Email Outreach | $49/user | $45/user | **Unlimited** |
| Sales Engagement | $99/user | $85/user | **Unlimited** |
| Agency | contact sales | — | Unlimited email addresses |

**Uploaded contacts are unlimited on every plan**, which is genuinely generous — many competitors meter exactly that.

Starter's 1,500 sends a month is the one hard volume cap, and it is about 50 a day. That is a research-and-personalise pace rather than a campaign pace: fine for a founder emailing carefully chosen prospects, insufficient for a sequence at scale. **The jump to unlimited sends costs $20 a user**, which is the best-value step on this page.

## The Agency plan is the tell

The Agency plan's stated benefit is **unlimited email addresses**.

That confirms the mailbox constraint is real and structural rather than incidental. Mailshake knows that heavy senders need many addresses, and it has built a tier that removes the one-mailbox-per-user rule — priced by sales conversation rather than published.

**If you are running outreach at any serious volume, that is the plan you will end up discussing**, and it is worth going into that conversation having done the seat arithmetic above so you know what the published alternative would have cost.

## Two add-ons, both published

- **Data Finder — $19/month** ($17 annual)
- **Ready to Use Mailboxes — $12/month** ($11 annual)

The second one is interesting in light of everything above: Mailshake will sell you the mailbox itself. At $12 a month plus the $49 seat it attaches to, a fully-provisioned extra sending address is **$61/month** — the honest per-address cost of scaling here.

Data Finder at $19 is contact discovery. The page does not state how many lookups that includes. **Ask**, because "a data add-on" with no stated volume is an open-ended line.

## Who should not buy this

**If you have not confirmed cold email is lawful for your market and list**, tooling is the wrong first question. Consent rules differ by jurisdiction and a platform that sends efficiently makes a compliance error efficiently too.

**If you need one mailbox and send fifty a month**, Starter at $25 annual is more infrastructure than the job needs, and a normal email client does it for nothing.

## The honest summary

**Unlimited contacts on every tier and a $20 step to unlimited sends are both genuinely good**, and the add-on prices are published rather than hidden.

**But price it per mailbox, not per person.** Each user is one sending address, so a careful one-person setup with four addresses is $196 a month at the middle tier — and if you are going wider than that, the Agency plan with unlimited addresses is the conversation you are actually heading for.
