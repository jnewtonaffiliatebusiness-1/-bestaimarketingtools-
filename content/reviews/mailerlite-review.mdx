## The comparison people are actually making

Most searches that land on a MailerLite review are really asking one question: **is this better than Mailchimp for what I need?** Usually at the free tier, usually for a list under a few hundred people.

So here is that comparison directly, from both companies' own pricing pages, checked August 2026.

| Free plan | MailerLite | Mailchimp |
|---|---|---|
| Subscribers | **250** | **250** |
| **Monthly sends** | **2,500** | **500** |
| Websites / landing pages | 1 + 1 | not included |
| User seats | 2 | — |
| Active forms | 3 | — |

**Identical subscriber ceiling. Five times the send allowance.**

## Why the send number is the one that matters

A 250-subscriber ceiling sounds like the binding constraint. In practice it usually is not — the send limit is.

Email 250 people once a week and you use **1,000 sends a month**. That is comfortable on MailerLite and **double Mailchimp's entire free allowance**. On Mailchimp's free plan you can email your full list twice a month and then you are done.

**Check your intended sending frequency before your list size.** It is the number that decides which free plan is usable, and it is the one nobody checks.

## What paid looks like

| Plan | Price | Sends |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | 2,500/mo |
| **Comfort** | **from $12/mo** | 10× your subscriber tier |
| **Power** | **from $25/mo** | **Unlimited** |
| Enterprise | custom | custom |

**Comfort's send allowance is a multiple, not a fixed number** — ten times whatever subscriber tier you are on. That is unusual and worth understanding: if you send frequently to a small list, you can hit the send ceiling while nowhere near the subscriber ceiling, and the fix is to climb a tier you did not otherwise need.

**Power at $25 removes the send limit entirely.** For anyone emailing daily, that is the tier the pricing model is quietly pushing you toward, and it is worth pricing directly rather than arriving at it by accident.

## Where Mailchimp still wins

Being fair about this matters more than the headline.

**Integrations.** Mailchimp's ecosystem is the widest in email marketing. If a tool you depend on has one email integration, it is probably Mailchimp. Check yours before switching on price.

**Familiarity.** If someone else will run your email, more people have used Mailchimp than anything else, and that is a real operational cost you avoid.

**Template depth.** Mailchimp's library is larger. If design variety matters to your brand, that is a genuine advantage.

## Where neither is the right tool

If you need **sales funnels, checkout and courses** as well as email, both of these are dedicated email senders and you are shopping in the wrong category. [Systeme.io's free plan runs 2,000 contacts with unlimited emails](/reviews/systeme-io-free-plan-limits) and includes funnels and a course platform — eight times the free subscribers of either tool here, with the trade-off that its email design tools are more basic and its page builder is rigid.

And if you are **publishing rather than selling**, [Kit's free plan carries 10,000 subscribers](/reviews/convertkit-review) — forty times either of these — with unlimited broadcasts but no automation until you pay.

## The honest summary

**On the narrow question of MailerLite versus Mailchimp at the free tier, MailerLite wins on the numbers** — same subscribers, five times the sends, a website and a landing page included, and a cheaper paid entry.

That is not the same as saying it is the better product for you. **Mailchimp's integration ecosystem is a real reason to pay more**, and it is the only reason that survives a look at the pricing pages.

*All figures from MailerLite's and Mailchimp's own pricing pages, checked August 2026.*
