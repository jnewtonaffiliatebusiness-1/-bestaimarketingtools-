## The trade, stated plainly

Almost every email tool in this category prices on two axes: **how many contacts you have** and **how many emails you send them**. Hit either ceiling and you upgrade.

GetResponse prices on one. From its own pricing page, checked August 2026:

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Contacts | Monthly sends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | **$19** | $15.58 | from 1,000 | **Unlimited** |
| **Marketer** | **$59** | $48.38 | from 1,000 | **Unlimited** |
| **Creator** | **$69** | $56.58 | from 1,000 | **Unlimited** |
| Enterprise | custom | custom | flexible | Unlimited |

**Unlimited sends on the $19 plan.** That is the whole proposition, and it is unusual enough to be the reason to consider the product.

The price of it: **there is no permanent free plan.** GetResponse offers a 14-day trial and then you pay.

## Who that trade suits

**Anyone who emails often.** If you send daily, or run a broadcast plus a nurture sequence plus a launch campaign in the same month, send limits are the constraint you keep bumping into elsewhere. [MailerLite's Comfort tier allows 10× your subscriber count](/reviews/mailerlite-review); [Mailchimp's Essentials allows 5,000 a month](/reviews/mailchimp-review). Both are generous until you send a lot, and then they are not.

**Anyone who has been surprised by an overage.** Mailchimp's pricing page states that overages apply when you exceed the contact or send limit. A plan with no send limit cannot generate that particular surprise.

**Anyone who wants one number to forecast.** Budgeting against contacts alone is simpler than budgeting against contacts and volume.

## Who it does not suit

**Anyone still validating.** Fourteen days is not long enough to find out whether an audience wants your emails. If you do not yet have a list, paying $19/month to build one from zero is the wrong order of operations — [Kit's free plan carries 10,000 subscribers](/reviews/convertkit-review) and costs nothing while you find out.

**Anyone who emails monthly.** Unlimited sending has no value to a low-frequency sender. You are buying insurance against a problem you do not have, at above the category's entry price.

## The gap that decides the real cost

Starter at $19 is the headline. **Marketer at $59 is where most people end up**, and the $40 gap is the number to look at rather than the entry price.

The article-writing exercise worth doing: list the automations you actually intend to build in your first ninety days. If any of them need more than basic sequences, you are pricing Marketer, and GetResponse's proposition should be compared at $59 rather than $19.

## What this does not do

GetResponse is a capable email platform with landing pages and automation attached. **It is not an all-in-one business platform.** If you need funnels, courses, checkout and affiliate management in the same tool, [Systeme.io runs $17–97 with unlimited emails on every tier including free](/reviews/systeme-io-free-plan-limits) — and unlimited sending is not a differentiator against it, because Systeme.io does that too, at a lower entry price, with a free tier attached.

Where GetResponse is genuinely stronger is email itself: deliverability tooling, segmentation depth, and a more capable editor.

## The honest summary

**If you send a lot of email to a modest list, GetResponse's single-axis pricing is a real advantage** and $19 with unlimited sends is a fair entry.

**If you send infrequently, or you have not built the list yet, the absence of a free plan is disqualifying** and the unlimited sending you are paying for is capacity you will never touch.

*All figures from GetResponse's own pricing page, checked August 2026. Prices shown are the 1,000-contact tier; all plans scale with list size.*
