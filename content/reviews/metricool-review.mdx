## There is no single Metricool price

Most pricing pages have one number per tier. This one has a slider, and the tier name is only half the answer.

| Plan | Brands | Price/month |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 1 | **$0** |
| Starter | 5 | **$20** |
| Starter | 10 | **$36** |
| Advanced | 15 | **$53** |
| Advanced | 25 | **$85** |
| Advanced | 50 | **$159** |

So "Starter" is not $20 — it is $20 *or* $36. "Advanced" is $53, $85 or $159. **The plan you pick sets the feature band; the brand count sets the bill.**

This is why quoted prices for this tool disagree with each other and with your invoice. Any comparison saying "Metricool starts at $20" is quoting the cheapest row of a six-row table.

## Per brand, it is very cheap — and that is the real story

Run the division:

- Starter, 5 brands: **$4.00 per brand**
- Starter, 10 brands: **$3.60**
- Advanced, 15 brands: **$3.53**
- Advanced, 25 brands: **$3.40**
- Advanced, 50 brands: **$3.18**

That curve is remarkably flat and remarkably low. For an agency, **$3.18 per brand per month at 50 brands** is aggressive pricing for scheduling plus analytics plus competitor tracking, and it is the honest reason this tool keeps appearing in agency stacks.

The flatness matters too: there is no punitive jump where the vendor knows you are locked in. You pay roughly the same per brand at 5 as at 50, which is unusual and worth crediting.

## The free plan is a real plan with one real limit

Metricool's free tier states:

- Manage **1 brand**
- Schedule up to **20 posts per month**
- Analyze **5 competitor profiles**
- Access **30 days of analytics**

Twenty posts a month is roughly five a week — enough to run one brand at a steady cadence, and genuinely usable rather than a demo.

**The limit that actually bites is 30 days of analytics.** Social performance is a trend question: is this working better than last quarter, did that format change anything, when did engagement start sliding. Thirty days cannot answer any of those. You get a snapshot and no direction, which is the one thing analytics is for.

**If you are on free and wondering whether to pay, that is the line to test yourself against** — not the post count.

## Competitor tracking is the upgrade nobody mentions

Free tracks **5 competitor profiles**. Paid tiers track **up to 100**.

That is a twentyfold increase and it changes what the tool is for. Five competitors is spot-checking. A hundred is category surveillance — enough to see what an entire vertical is posting, which formats are gaining, and which accounts are actually growing rather than merely large.

For an agency pitching new business, that data is arguably worth more than the scheduling. It is the strongest argument for the paid tiers and it is buried under the brand-count slider on the pricing page.

## Two numbers to get in writing

**Users per plan are not stated** — not on Free, Starter, Advanced or Custom. For a tool an agency team shares, how many people can log in is not a detail, and its absence is the biggest gap on the page.

**The monthly-versus-annual presentation is contradictory as published.** The page states annual billing saves up to 24%, while the annual column shows a *higher* per-month figure than the monthly column. Both cannot be true, and rather than guess which way round it is, **check the billing toggle yourself at checkout.** Every per-brand figure above is taken from the ladder as listed; the direction of the annual discount is not something this page settles.

## Who should not buy this

**If you run one brand and post a few times a week**, the free plan covers you and the correct spend is zero until the 30-day analytics window starts costing you decisions.

**If you want a tool that tells you what to post**, this is not that. It schedules, measures and watches competitors — it is an instrument panel, and an instrument panel does not fly the plane.

## The honest summary

**Priced per brand, this is among the cheapest serious options in the category, and the ladder is published in full rather than hidden behind a call.** At $3-something per brand it is easy to justify for an agency.

**Just do not trust any single quoted price, including a $20 one.** Count your brands, read across to your row, confirm how many users the plan allows, and check the annual toggle at checkout — because the page as published contradicts itself on that one point.

*The free-plan limits (1 brand, 20 posts/month, 5 competitor profiles, 30 days of analytics), the Starter and Advanced brand-count price ladders in USD, and the up-to-100 competitor tracking on paid tiers are from Metricool's own pricing page, checked 11 August 2026. Users per plan are not stated on that page, and the monthly/annual figures as presented conflict with the stated saving.*
